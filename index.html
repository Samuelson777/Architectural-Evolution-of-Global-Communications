Executive Abstract Author: SAMUELSON G

Global telecommunications and data networking infrastructures are undergoing a foundational architectural transformation driven by the convergence of native IPv6 network programmability, disaggregated wireless control planes, ultra-low-latency storage fabrics, post-quantum cryptographic migration, and quantum mechanical information transport. Classical networking paradigms, historically reliant on rigid protocol stacks and stateful multiprotocol signaling, are increasingly unable to satisfy the deterministic latency, extreme connection density, and cryptographic resilience demanded by distributed artificial intelligence, immersive mixed reality, and autonomous industrial systems.

This paper presents a comprehensive, cross-disciplinary architectural analysis of contemporary network taxonomies and their evolutionary trajectory toward 2030 and beyond. At the classical core and transport layers, the transition from Multiprotocol Label Switching (MPLS) to Segment Routing over IPv6 (SRv6, RFC 8986) embeds stateless network instructions directly within 128-bit IPv6 addresses, unifying traffic engineering, Virtual Private Networks (VPNs), and Service Function Chaining (SFC) under a single forwarding plane. Across access and data center domains, Wireless Local Area Networks (WLANs) are advancing through IEEE 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7) Extremely High Throughput (EHT) specifications, Storage Area Networks (SANs) are replacing legacy SCSI-over-Fibre Channel translation with Non-Volatile Memory Express over Fabrics (NVMe-oF) utilizing Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA), and cellular Radio Access Networks (RANs) are disaggregating via Open RAN (O-RAN) multi-timescale intelligent controllers.

Looking toward the next decade, the International Telecommunication Union (ITU-R) IMT-2030 framework for sixth-generation (6G) mobile systems establishes stringent performance boundaries—including 0.1 to 1 ms air-interface latency, 106 to 108 devices/km2 connection density, and centimeter-level positioning—while integrating sensing and AI natively into the network fabric. Concurrently, the existential threat posed by quantum algorithms to classical asymmetric cryptography has catalyzed the standardization of Module-Lattice and Stateless Hash-Based Post-Quantum Cryptography (NIST FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205). Ultimately, the emergence of the Quantum Internet—operating via quantum superposition, multipartite entanglement, quantum repeaters, and quantum teleportation—establishes an entirely new physical and logical communication paradigm capable of information-theoretic security and distributed quantum computation toward the 7G horizon.

1. Foundational Network Architectures and Taxonomy of Contemporary Networks

Modern communication networks are structured around the logical separation of operational planes—specifically the Forwarding Plane (Data Plane), Control Plane, Management Plane, and Application Plane (IETF RFC 7426). Rather than viewing network types merely as geographical classifications (LAN, SAN, RAN, WAN), modern systems engineering evaluates network taxonomies by how each domain manages protocol overhead, state distribution, and hardware disaggregation.

3. High-Performance Access, Storage Fabrics, and Disaggregated Radio Networks

Wi-Fi 7 (IEEE 802.11be)

Introduces Multi-Link Operation (MLO) at the MAC layer, allowing clients to aggregate 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz spectrum simultaneously for sub-5ms latency and ultra-high throughput.

NVMe over Fabrics (NVMe-oF)

Replaces legacy SCSI-to-NVMe translation with native NVMe command queues over RDMA (RoCE), Fibre Channel (FC-NVMe), or TCP, achieving microsecond SAN fabric latency.

Open RAN (O-RAN)

Disaggregates base stations into O-CU, O-DU, and O-RU, using Near-RT RIC (10ms-1s control loops) with ML xApps and A1/E2 open interfaces to eliminate vendor lock-in.

4. Next-Generation Ubiquitous Connectivity: The IMT-2030 (6G) Framework

Finalized in Recommendation ITU-R M.2160-0 (November 2023), the IMT-2030 framework defines 6G specifications, targeting air-interface latencies of 0.1 to 1 ms, device density of 108 devices/km2, and Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC).

6G Performance Targets vs. 5G Baseline

Target Spectrum Allocations

Studies prioritize sub-6GHz and upper mid-band (FR3: 7.125–8.4 GHz) with 1.275 GHz contiguous bandwidth alongside mmWave (37–37.6 GHz) for optimal macro-coverage and spatial resolution.

Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC)

Waveforms function as radar transceivers delivering 1–10 cm 3D spatial positioning and object tracking without requiring active transponders.

7. Synthesis and Architectural Conclusions

A rigorous cross-layer synthesis of contemporary and emerging communication networks reveals three overarching architectural trajectories that will govern global infrastructure engineering over the next two decades. First, classical networking is converging onto stateless, header-programmed forwarding planes and disaggregated intelligent control loops. In wide-area transport, Segment Routing over IPv6 (SRv6, RFC 8986) eliminates the multi-protocol complexity of MPLS by encoding topological paths and VPN table lookups directly into 128-bit IPv6 SIDs.

Second, the boundary between communication, sensing, and computation is dissolving under the IMT-2030 (6G) framework. By targeting sub-millisecond air-interface latency, 108 devices/km2 connection density, and 1–10 cm 3D positioning across upper mid-band (7.125–8.4 GHz) spectrum, 6G transforms the wireless interface into an Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) radar and distributed AI execution substrate.

Third, quantum physics is simultaneously disrupting classical network cryptography and birthing a complementary quantum communication plane. Ultimately, the realization of the Quantum Internet will not replace the classical Internet; because quantum teleportation inherently depends on classical signaling channels to transmit Bell-state measurement outcomes, the future global information infrastructure will operate as a hybrid classical-quantum continuum.
